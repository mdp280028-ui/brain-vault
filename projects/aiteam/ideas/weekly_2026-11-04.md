## Stop slug_already_shipped skips at the source: pre-filter queue, not just dedupe logs

**Score:** 3.600000000000000088 · **Build:** 2.0h · **Impact:** `medium` · **Slug:** `ship-to-site-skip-dedup-third-recurrence-write-guard`

**Signal:** `diagnose.e_repeated_failures_14d[0] (ship_to_site_skipped, n=572) and a_top_actions_7d[2..9]`

ship_to_site_skipped is still the dominant row in the audit_log: 572 events in 14 days, and 8 of the top 10 7d actions are ship_to_site_skipped at 14 each across distinct slugs (a_top_actions_7d, e_repeated_failures_14d[0]). The example payload is reason=slug_already_shipped for a slug already in APPROVED_GUIDE_SLUGS. Two prior proposals (ship-to-site-skip-dedup, ship-to-site-skip-dedup-recurrence) targeted dedup of the log writes; both are still pending and the volume has grown, not shrunk.

This idea uses a different mechanism: remove already-shipped slugs from the candidate list before ship-to-site iterates it, so the skip event never fires. A single pre-pass compares the queue against APPROVED_GUIDE_SLUGS and drops matches, logging one summary row per tick (count dropped) instead of one row per slug. Each slug is currently logged 14 times a week, which is once per two-hour tick or similar, so the pre-filter removes roughly 570 rows per 14 days and makes the audit_log signal usable again for the diagnose queries that read it.

Productivity number: this keeps e_repeated_failures_14d from being dominated by a non-failure, which frees ~0.5 operator hours/week of triage noise and lets real repeated failures (srcver_fail at 60) surface in the top rows. Downside: if the pre-filter has a bug and drops slugs that were NOT actually shipped, they silently never ship. Mitigate with the summary row listing dropped slugs.
