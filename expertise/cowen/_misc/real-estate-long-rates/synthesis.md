## Current Thesis

Cowen argues that the decades-long era of falling long-term rates may have ended, and now puts a number on it: the 10-year Treasury yield rising to about 6.5% over the next four years, as part of a possible multi-decade rising-rate cycle. He sees a lost-decade risk for speculative assets and a stock-market crash likely over the next two years. He sees bonds and inflation-protected Treasuries as attractive over a 2–3 year horizon, and he has recently bought them. Real-estate sensitivity is implied, not argued directly: a guest notes housing developers are declining, and the cheap-financing tailwind is expected to fade. Cowen still frames the rate view as a long-run scenario rather than a certain call.
