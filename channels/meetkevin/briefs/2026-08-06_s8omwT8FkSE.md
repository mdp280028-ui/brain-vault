## TL;DR
Kevin argues an eerie timing parallel to the 2000 dot-com rate-hike cycle (roughly 38 months since the Fed's last hike, echoing the "nail in the coffin" hike then) means markets should be on alert for a possible September rate hike, with tomorrow's jobs report and the August 27-29 Jackson Hole meeting as the next key catalysts.

## Main points
1. Historical parallel: the dot-com bubble burst after the Fed resumed hiking after 2 years 3 months, hiking six times (June/Aug/Nov 1999, Feb/Mar/May 2000) for a total +1.75% (4.75%→6.5%); the market topped in March 2000, about 9 months after the first hike in that cycle. The final ("nail in the coffin") hike came 38 months after the Fed's prior hike before that — and if the Fed hikes September 16, 2026, it would also be exactly 38 months since the last hike, which Kevin calls "eerie" timing, though he caveats it's "not a perfect overlap."
2. Fed governor Kevin Walsh is reportedly acknowledging mistakes from his first 10 weeks (per a Financial Times piece), particularly his "left of the decimal" messaging, which Kevin says spooked the bond market (2-year/10-year yields spiked) and forced Walsh into a "damage control" posture that raises the odds of a hike.
3. Multiple Fed officials/voices are now leaning hawkish: Kashkari says rates don't sound restrictive given strong labor, corporate earnings, and Hormuz/Iran-driven energy uncertainty; Hammack, Logan, and Mary Daily are also flagged as warning on inflation/hikes; Bank of America is calling for three hikes this year (Sept, Oct, Dec).
4. The FT piece states Walsh "would be prepared to raise interest rates at September's meeting if inflation readings released in coming weeks are hot" — Kevin treats this as the key line, effectively a return to forward guidance despite official "no forward guidance" messaging, and flags it as a flip-flop.
5. Funding-cost/capex risk parallel to 2000: back then Greenspan's 1.75% of hikes coincided with treasuries at a 5-year high, preceding the NASDAQ 100's 78% crash (March 2000–Oct 2002, ~2.5 years) that hit Cisco, Intel, and Oracle hardest. Today, the 30-year Treasury hit ~5.27% after the July Fed meeting (a 20-22 year high, back to ~2004-2006 pricing), and AI-infra-adjacent borrowers are already paying up: CoreWeave raised debt above 10% for data centers, SpaceX had to raise rates to fill a bond offering, and Google is raising another $25B in a laddered bond offering (on top of a previously flagged $40B share sale not yet executed as of June 30). Kevin's point: rising funding costs make current capex cycles "less sustainable," not a certain crash call.
6. In the Alpha Report (his paid service) the prior day, Kevin flagged caution after "two epic days of releveraging" (Mon/Tue) ahead of Friday's jobs report; the market fell from the 725 level to 715 as he anticipated, and he says tomorrow's jobs print is now "very important," made more so by the Walsh FT story.
7. Treasury Secretary Bessent criticized WSJ reporter Nick Timiraos ("Nikki Leaks") for relying on Fed "backroom gossip," which Kevin says is ironic given the FT piece itself reads like forward guidance returning; Kevin says he trusts Timiraos's reporting/leaks as useful signal.
8. Kevin notes Bessent's 2024 campaign "3-3-3" agenda (3% growth, 3% deficit/GDP, +3M barrels oil/day) has missed on all fronts: growth ~1.9% annualized, deficit ~5.8% of GDP (as of September), oil production up only ~0.57M barrels/day over 18 months since Jan 2025 — "the only thing with a three in front of it is inflation."
