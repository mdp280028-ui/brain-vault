## TL;DR
Kevin says the sell-off is a leverage flush, not a recession. Anthropic holding in a 10–20% drop from its IPO price is his sign of that, and he says buy the dip, though he can't call the bottom. He holds Broadcom as his way into Anthropic.

## Main points
1. Anthropic, closed about $84, held its 10–20% decline range for two days after briefly dipping below -20%. He reads that as leverage being flushed out, not a recession. Anthropic has no leveraged ETFs, few options and little margin. He says a $40 price would signal recession.
2. He lists the bearish catalysts: institutions, elections, Iran, the Fed, tariffs and EU tariffs, a possible 50 bps Fed hike, Jensen, and Chinese chip exports. He says the list is bad but already known. He says he is happy to buy the sale and that people who say "bull trap" can't cite fundamentals.
3. Broadcom is his Anthropic proxy, and he owns it. He cites about $32B of cash flow over nine months, or about $42.6B annualized. That is about 3% free cash flow yield on a roughly $1.4T market cap. He puts the price near $300 on $11.67 EPS, a 25.5 P/E and 43% growth to 2030, a PEG of 0.59. Revenue nearly doubled and gross margin doubled. He says to diversify across the sector and not go all in.
4. Google moving from inference back to training chips means more chip spending, which he calls good for the sector. He says the "chip glut" fear is overdone, and he dismisses SemiAnalysis's reaction as emotional.
5. The Nasdaq nearly recovered a drop of over 4%. It bounced off his 607 line. He averaged the two bounces, 606.23 and 607.76, to get 607. Bitcoin is back around $60K. Rocket Mortgage rose 7% after an Alpha Report call tied to falling yields. Loan Depot rose 17% and Axon 7%. Lennar gained 3%.
6. Nvidia: S&P put its AA rating on CreditWatch negative over governance, and says a downgrade is possible within 90 days. Its 5-year CDS is 142, above Meta's 118. He says it "deserves" the lower rating and expects board members may be dismissed. VP Vance said Jensen is not under investigation.
7. Macro: Q3 GDP revised to 4.3% from 3.4–3.5%, and personal consumption was 4.2% vs 3.9%. Jobless claims are very low, the 4-week average is low and continuing claims fell. Building permits fell 7.2% year over year, twice as bad as expected. Atlanta Fed Q4 GDPNow is 2.9%. The GDP price index was 1.9% vs 2.3% expected. Kalshi shows a 6% chance of a Fed pause, and recession odds are low. The 2-year yield is steady near 4.6%. He says economic pain for low-income households is real, but the data does not point to recession.
8. Oil: the Houthis hit a Saudi Aramco facility. WTI is up about 3.6% to $94, which he calls "not ideal". CoreWeave CDS is elevated but not extreme, at 130 vs 1159 in October.
9. Housing: falling permits mean tighter supply through the 2028 elections and worse affordability. That is good for owners, and lower rates would lift transaction volume. He sees agent and lender side jobs as a good idea, and says his company got an NMLS mortgage broker license. He thinks tariffs get repealed by 2029 and inflation turns negative, which helps stocks and real estate.
10. SpaceX and Tesla: SpaceX plans one share per active Trump account. He thinks the Tesla/SpaceX merger is likely, and Kalshi odds are about a third by January and rising for year-end. The FCC approved 1M satellites for orbital compute.
11. Positioning: he holds a basket of about 34 AI stocks. He holds Anthropic only through perpetuals bought with $5K of sponsor money. He has not added Corning yet. He says the AI bubble "is ahead, not behind us". He tells people to invest only money they can afford to lose, and to use less leverage. Promotion and the 1Password hack segment are skipped.
