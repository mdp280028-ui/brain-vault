## TL;DR
Kevin argues SpaceX's AI-compute economics look like a statistical anomaly built on misleading marketing and a circular web of incentives between SpaceX, Google, and Anthropic — making the stock riskier, not a "we're ready to rock" story.

## Main points
1. SpaceX claims it builds data center compute (Colossus) at $2.7M/megawatt while earning ~$30M/megawatt annually — roughly 1/5 the cost and 6x the revenue of peers (Nebius $4.5M, Core Scientific $1.75M, CoreWeave $1.65M, hyperscaler average $2.3M), an outlier he calls a "statistical anomaly."
2. The $2.7M figure is an unaudited management claim covering only the brownfield shell (old factories converted, built in 91 days) with no GPUs; Deutsche Bank estimates fully equipped cost at $25–30M/megawatt, in line with industry — so the "cheap to build" marketing is misleading.
3. The premium revenue is explained by conflicts of interest: Google holds $94.1B in SpaceX shares ($80B short-term lockup, $14.1B long-term) and $124B in Anthropic; Google's SpaceX stake appreciation was the majority of its net income last quarter (its first cash-flow-negative quarter). Anthropic rents Colossus 1 (100%) and part of Colossus 2 at $30M/megawatt/year (6x hyperscaler rates); Google signed at $50M/megawatt/year (67% premium to Anthropic's already-inflated rate) — Kevin frames this as a "circle jerk" where overpaying for compute pumps SpaceX stock, which benefits Google, which benefits Anthropic via Google's stake.
4. Cancellation risk: Anthropic and Google both have 90-day rolling cancellation rights after initial waiting periods (ending by year-end 2026); Google can cancel early if SpaceX fails to deliver functional access by September 30, 2026 (56 days out). Elon is already citing a memory-chip shortage (memory output growing 20%/year vs. ~200%/year demand growth, RAM prices up 4x YoY) as a potential force-majeure excuse for delivery delays.
5. CapEx math: 220,000 GB300 chips + 800 NICs per facility = ~3,056 racks at $10M each (~$30B), plus ~$1.5B InfiniBand, $4–8B facility buildout, $1.5B storage ≈ $40B per facility, ~$80B for both "micro hard" and "mini/macro hard" builds — a bet that only pays back in under a year if SpaceX keeps getting these above-market rates, which "nobody else is being charged and nobody else is charging."
6. AI revenue jumped from $737M to $2.5B, boosted by a suspiciously flat $1.6B "cloud services" figure likely from one or two customer deposits (probably Anthropic/Google); AI infra was 85.8% of $18.4B in Q2 capex (only 14.2% went to rockets); SpaceX burned ~$5B over 6 months and ~$25B negative cash flow over 25 months, but raised ~$85B in its "the suckening" round plus debt/credit facilities, leaving ~$50B free cash — enough for roughly 6–9 months of capex.
7. Peer comparison: CoreWeave — $7.7B quarterly capex, -$4.7B free cash flow, $740M net loss, $4.3B cash vs. $17.3B long-term debt (~$15.1B short-term bills, 2.5x cash on hand), had to raise debt at 10%+ yield (1.25 points above WSJ's expected rate); up to 89% of receivables reportedly from just Microsoft, OpenAI, Google. Nebius is healthier: $9.3B cash, $1.3B current liabilities, $8.4B long-term debt, still spending nearly all cash flow (~$2.5B) and raising via notes/warrants/Nvidia investment.
8. Rocket-side notes (secondary): Elon targets one Starship flight/day eventually (2025 cadence was ~1 every other day) — Kevin discounts Musk timelines by "adding multiple" to them; Falcon is no longer taking new orders and will fly through 2029 (~70 more flights by 2028) as SpaceX shifts focus to Starship (next test flight 14); V3 Starlink satellites promise ~10x faster service. Kevin reiterates his pre-IPO call of a ~30% run-up followed by a bleed-out, which he says has played out so far, and says the AI compute pivot makes the stock "even more risky" given poor visibility into real contract terms.
