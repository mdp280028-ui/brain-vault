## TL;DR
Bullish on Nvidia after a beat and raise (revenue $115.8B vs his $111B guess, guide $130B ±3%), with the SEC probe treated as a manageable overhang. He says the next catalysts are the Anthropic IPO pricing and the Fed in about three weeks, and urges patience plus diversification.

## Main points
1. Nvidia revenue was $115.8B, 5.9% above analyst estimates and above his $111B call. Guidance is $130B ±3%, against his $120B ±2%. Revenue grew about 2.03x while cost of revenue grew about 95%, and operating expenses rose only 56%, which he reads as pricing power. Operating cash flow was $48B for the quarter, with $178B in cash, equivalents and receivables against about $100B of debt.
2. Supply and capacity commitments rose to $390B, up $111B (about 39%) in one quarter. Vera Rubin began shipping in Q3 and was only $20B of the $107B data center revenue, which he calls the moat. Nvidia added $150B to its buyback and cut purchases of other companies' debt 48%, to $11.4B. He reads that as management saying its own stock is cheap. He bought the dip that morning.
3. The SEC is reportedly investigating Nvidia, per WSJ, and the reason is unclear. He expects it concerns whether disclosures misled investors. He notes the 10-Q already carries a generic compliance-violation risk disclosure. He sees it as an extra catalyst, not a thesis breaker.
4. Macro overhang: no Iran deal, Fed rate hikes, Democratic wins (Nomura says the AI buildout may be slower), and a new ban on Chinese chips that could open licensing avenues. Morgan Stanley is still bullish on hyperscalers, and so is he. He hopes the Anthropic IPO and Nvidia guidance clear the catalysts through the next Fed meeting.
5. Nvidia CEO succession: he holds a small Kalshi position on Jensen returning as CEO, which is at 13%. He says Rene Haas (Arm) and Ian Buck are likelier candidates.
