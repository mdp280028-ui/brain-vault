## TL;DR
Kevin argues the economy is being "papered over" by AI-driven stock-market wealth effects and warns of an eventual AI-spending cycle collapse; he stays bearish on commoditized data-center/hyperscaler names (Oracle, Coreweave, SpaceX-as-Neocloud) while remaining bullish on pricing-power plays like Nvidia (near-term), Meta, and Palantir/Axon.

## Main points
1. Kevin says the -23K jobs print will likely be revised down further, but argues employment is a lagging indicator — bad jobs data no longer moves markets because AI hype, stock-market wealth effects, and heavy consumer/retail spending (alongside falling personal savings) are "papering over" an economy that is "bleeding out slowly" from tariffs, oil, war, and private-credit failures (he cites another private-credit firm failing "today").
2. His recession trigger thesis: not the labor market, but an eventual stop in AI spending — he floats a scenario where Anthropic/OpenAI IPO and their stocks tank (referencing SpaceX falling 50% in its first month post-listing as a precedent), cutting off capital for data-center buildout and causing the "AI cycle" to "screech to a halt," which he calls the real recession catalyst; he says he expects Fed chair-designate Kevin Warsh to only cut rates to zero rather than provide a broader bailout.
3. Kevin says he personally avoids data-center/hyperscaler and "compute commodity" stocks long-term because they lack pricing power — compute is becoming a commodity like water, where price converges once shortage ends (he cites GB300 pricing ranging ~$11/hr down to ~$6/hr for bulk, implying no durable pricing power once supply catches up).
4. He walks Oracle's balance sheet live: ~$31B cash vs. ~$31.8B near-term bills payable (barely covers bills), plus ~$122B in additional debt (excluding leases), ~$150B total long-term-debt-adjacent liabilities against a ~$435B market cap; operating income ~$20.6B against ~$5.6B interest expense; operating cash flow ~$32B vs. ~$55B spent, requiring ~$46B in new debt to cover the gap — he calls this a warning sign and says "SpaceX is going to end up doing this," expecting SpaceX to take on similarly large debt.
5. He reiterates dislike of AI-infrastructure/"crypto-turned-datacenter" plays (names NBIS, Iren) long-term, calling them likely "bag holders" once compute commoditizes; also referenced Riot's $9B compute deal with Anthropic as evidence buyers don't care whose chips they use, only that compute is available.
6. Nvidia PEG/KPEG walkthrough: using ~$9 EPS and ~$217.55 share price, Kevin calculates a PE of ~24; using Wall Street's 4-year forward growth estimates (43.4%, 24%, 91%(sic), then -22.6%) he gets an average forward growth rate of ~33.9%, yielding a PEG near/below 1 (cheap) — but he warns that if growth estimates roll over to flat/negative (e.g., -10, 11, 0, 0 → ~0.25% average), the same math produces a PEG of ~96, making the stock "off the chart" expensive; he says this rolling-over growth trajectory is why he's cautious on data-center-linked names over a 5-10 year horizon.
7. Kevin reaffirms he's bullish Meta into year-end (price target ~$1,377), framing it primarily as an AI-enhanced-advertising beneficiary (3B daily active users) rather than a data-center-capex risk, and says Zuckerberg is smartly pivoting away from being a "Frontier Lab" toward monetizing users via ads while giving away LLM technology.
8. On Anduril/OpenAI potential IPOs: Kevin says Anduril will likely be "a bitching company," is more lukewarm on OpenAI, and stresses that any IPO decision should wait for financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement) rather than hype.
