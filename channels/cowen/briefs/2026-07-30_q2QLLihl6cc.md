## Key points (the distilled video)
1. June PCE price index fell 0.1% month-over-month (3.7% year-over-year); core PCE rose 0.1%; GDP grew at an annualized 1.5%; initial jobless claims at 197,000 — Bitcoin ticked up modestly on the release.
2. Guy summarizes three concerning signals from Fed's Kevin Walsh: (1) no forward guidance going forward — "play the ball and not the referee" — meaning markets must guess Fed moves until announced, raising uncertainty/volatility; (2) three of twelve FOMC members dissented in favor of raising rates now, and Walsh called this "the beginning of the story and not the end"; (3) the Fed remains fixed on the 2% inflation target regardless of pain caused, despite the public having adjusted to ~3% as a new normal ("shifting baseline theory").
3. Fed funds rate target held at 3.50-3.75%; next meeting September 16, 2026 is pricing roughly a 60/40 split toward a hike to 3.75-4.00%.
4. Ben notes the Fed funds rate was recently above the 2-year yield (restrictive), but has now flipped so the 2-year yield sits above Fed funds — implying policy is no longer restrictive, which could let the labor market and wage/headline inflation reheat.
5. Ben points to the 30-year yield breaking to new highs and the 10-year approaching its October 2023 level; back in 2023, yields broke out starting July and rose into October, and the S&P dropped about 10% during that stretch — he sees a similar setup risk now and expects a rate hike this year (September a likely candidate).
6. The group's preferred narrative: a stock market correction in the back half of the midterm year coincides with a Bitcoin cycle bottom, driven by fear over Fed rate hikes — consistent with prior seasonality discussions.
7. A doveish (unnamed) Fed member said on Squawk Box that "even I can see a case for raising rates," reinforcing the panel's view that further easing/dovishness is not guaranteed.
8. Anecdote: a farmer who bought BTC in 2013 sold his truck for $55K and lump-summed it into Bitcoin, then took a 6.5%, five-year loan to buy a replacement truck; Guy says he's uncomfortable with using borrowed money for a volatile asset, though timing (buying now, expecting a possible year-end price rise) could work out; Ben says the outcome hinges on the buyer's holding timeline.
9. Ben says he prefers DCA into Bitcoin over lump-sum, noting Bitcoin can drop ~50% in ways the stock market doesn't overnight; he expects "a few more scary times this year" before things improve.
10. On whether the Bitcoin bottom is already in: Ben pegs it at "probably less than 50%" likelihood, expecting the most likely bottom timing to be Q4 of this year, consistent with seasonal patterns from February and earlier-year lows this cycle.
11. On Strategy (MSTR) not buying dips: Guy attributes this to cash-flow strain from dividend obligations on preferred stock (STRC), forcing them to choose between diluting MSTR holders or selling Bitcoin rather than accumulating; he expects smaller/weaker digital-asset-treasury companies to keep failing and release Bitcoin back to the market, calling it a long-term bullish "treasury companies will bleed to Bitcoin" dynamic. Cowen corrects an earlier claim, noting Strategy actually bought at $117K, not "the high."
12. Hyperliquid (HYPE) discussed briefly by Guy as one of few altcoins with real product-market fit and strong revenue, though facing rising competition (e.g., Lighter); he expects it to hold up long-term absent a hack/exploit.

## Predictions made
- Ben expects a Fed rate hike this year, with September as a likely candidate month.
- Ben estimates less than 50% chance the Bitcoin cycle bottom is already in, with Q4 of this year as the most likely bottom window.
