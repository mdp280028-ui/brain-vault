## TL;DR
Kevin raises his Palantir year-end price target from $192 to $202 (up to $235 on a richer multiple) after the stock's 29% surge to $160, arguing the fundamentals (accelerating commercial growth, soaring net dollar retention) justify the move even though the stock isn't cheap on a cash flow basis.

## Main points
1. Palantir grew revenue 93% YoY to ~$2B/quarter; government growth was ~79% (only 1pt faster than last year's 78%) but commercial growth accelerated 7 points to 110% (from 103%), which Kevin calls "growing on top of growing."
2. Palantir's market cap ($390B) is more than double Salesforce's ($156B) despite Salesforce doing ~$11.1B/quarter in revenue vs. Palantir's ~$2B, because the market trusts Palantir's growth (93%) as resilient while doubting Salesforce's 13.2% growth.
3. Customer count growth is decelerating (from 8-13% quarterly growth down to 4-5.5%), which looks bearish at first glance, but net dollar retention (NDR) is "skyrocketing" — from ~111-120% in 2024, to ~130% in 2025, to ~150% in 2026 so far — because Palantir is selectively adding fewer new customers while selling existing ones much more (average commercial customer spend ~$615K/month; minimum bar to even get a sales call is ~$600K/month, with a realistic floor of $250-300K/month even excluding top 20 contracts).
4. Kevin argues Palantir's government work (including "clandestine" institutions/anti-terrorism messaging per CEO Karp) functions as marketing that builds trust for the much larger commercial/enterprise opportunity, and that Palantir's willingness to turn away customers (e.g., law firms who can't get access after seeing Kirkland & Ellis use it) creates a self-reinforcing exclusivity moat.
5. Rule of 40 (margin + growth) sits at 155% for Palantir, with 73 deals completed at $10M+; pricing power is strong — revenue grew ~93% while costs rose only 53%, sales & marketing grew 39%, G&A 19.6%, R&D 42.6% — all fractions of the growth rate.
6. Valuation concern (the "red flag"): Palantir's free cash flow yield is only ~1% (~$4B annual cash flow / $400B market cap) vs. Salesforce's 10.8% (~$17B cash flow / $156B market cap) — Kevin says the market may be overvaluing Palantir's moat somewhat, calling the stock "a little rich."
7. Balance sheet: ~$9B in cash (net of ~$500M in bills/lease obligations) plus ~$4B/year in free cash flow; CapEx is minimal (~$22M/quarter) so no AI-infrastructure overspending risk; no buybacks currently but Kevin expects them to resume given the cash pile.
8. New year-end price target: $202 (up from prior $192) using updated EPS ($1.57 forecast) and growth (~48% avg) at a PEG of 2.157 (current PE 103); a more generous 3x PEG at 50% growth could justify $235. Longer-term, even assuming growth decelerates to 25% by 2030 ($7.48 EPS), a 2.69 PEG gives a $503 target and a 3x PEG gives $561 — roughly 4.7x from his $118 purchase price last week, or 3.4x from today's ~$165.
