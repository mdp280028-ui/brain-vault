## TL;DR
Kevin says a December Fed hike, the hot CPI and the lack of an Iran deal are already priced in, so the market is moving from a high-catalyst period to a low-catalyst one. He is buying dips, starting with the beaten-down tech names in his 200-day moving average basket.

## Main points
1. Pricing: the market prices a >100% chance of a December hike (1.13 hikes), a second in March and a third by September, and he says the poor structure came from stacked catalysts (Iran threats, no deal, election, CPI). He expects a low-catalyst stretch, with only ISM, PMI and PPI on Friday ahead. He thinks PPI can only bring good news because the bad news is priced in.
2. Iran: Kalshi odds of a US-Iran nuclear deal by March 1, 2027 have fallen to 15%. Rubio told G7 ministers the US will do nothing and keep the blockade, with no "false time pressure." Kevin says the best time for a deal was before the election, so deal odds have dropped. He expects an eternal war but says the economy will absorb it.
3. Oil: Saudi output in October was its highest since before the war began in late February. Brent fell about 20% from the high, which he reads as oil having peaked. Doing nothing means oil falls slowly. He disagrees with the Fed that energy prices would take time to normalize after a deal and expects oil to fall quickly on a deal.
4. Tech rebound: his 200-day moving average basket rose sharply today. SanDisk +16%, Micron +12%, SK Hynix +10%, Seagate +11%, Marvell +12%, Lumentum +15%, with AMD -7%. He calls tech "ridiculously oversold" and expects the rebound to continue even with no deal and a hike. With a deal, he says the QQQ would reach 800 quickly.
5. AI positioning: hedge fund AI exposure fell to 32% of portfolios from 47% in June, and leverage dropped from the 99th percentile. Credit default swaps on CoreWeave, Meta and Oracle have peaked and are falling, which he calls a leading indicator for recovery. He says the real AI euphoria is ahead of us, not behind us, and that he is buying.
6. The Fed letter: Bowman named AI and energy prices as the two issues driving the mandate. In her framing, about 4% inflation splits into roughly 1 point baseline, 1 point tariffs, 1 point Iran/oil and 1 point AI. Kevin reads this as the Fed telling Trump that an Iran deal would remove the need for hikes. Tighter financial conditions, she says, make the market more discerning. He adds that a stock rally effectively guarantees a hike, but that hike is already priced in.
7. CoreWeave and Anthropic: CoreWeave rose 22% after saying it will not use unsecured debt at current yields and will fund growth through delayed-draw loans and customer prepayments (Nvidia, Microsoft). He says this sets a floor. Anthropic IPO bids are about $1.4T versus OpenAI at $1.25T, with OpenAI ARR of $40B (revenue up 50% in a quarter), and he says Anthropic's ARR is about twice as big.
8. Labor and Warsh: Bowman says labor risks have improved, with layoffs down, which gives room to focus on inflation. Kevin expects Warsh to use a labor-market task force to argue for caution on hikes, but thinks a December hike is still likely without an Iran deal. He puts no weight on AI-driven job loss in the near term.
