## TL;DR
Kevin argues SpaceX's technical bounce after its first lockup expiration is misleading because the real lock-up selling pressure (share restriction stamps, transfer-agent processing) hasn't actually started, and separately warns that SpaceX's compute rental rates from Anthropic/Google (3-5x the market rate) may not be a sustainable benchmark, making Neoclouds like Iron and Coreweave the potentially better-positioned trade if rates converge.

## Main points
1. Terafab (Tesla/SpaceX chip fab in Grimes County, Texas, 100 million sq ft, $55-119 billion projected investment) includes an Elon-confirmed "free electron laser lithography" ring meant to etch wafers beyond ASML's current capability — Kevin calls this a "2030 call option," citing test patches expected in 2027 and ramp in H2 2027, plus risk from Governor Abbott's Texas-wide data center audit (no approvals until complete) and natural gas power buildout timelines (3-4 years for real peaker plants vs. 3-4 months if using Bloom Energy units).
2. Terafab output is expected ~80% for orbital/space data center chips (D3 chips, used in satellites) and ~20% for Optimus/FSD (AI6 chips) — distinct from Macro/Micro Hard, which are Earth-based facilities expected 2027-2028 using mostly Nvidia GB300/Rubin chips; Elon confirmed SpaceX will buy more Nvidia chips, which Kevin says is bullish for NVDA (near all-time highs, could reach $300 if growth holds) and bearish-reactive for AMD today.
3. SpaceX's first lockup (4.9% to 11.8% of float, occurring Thursday) did not trigger a dump as many feared; stock bottomed the day before, rose 6% on the lockup day, then ~11% more the next day. Kevin says he called this in advance on his live stream, arguing the news was priced in and shares aren't as easy to sell as people assume.
4. Reason lockup selling hasn't materialized: shares need the restriction stamp removed from certificates via the transfer agent (process started only yesterday), then delivered to fund managers, then to end-investor brokerage accounts — a multi-step process with no known timeline. Free float is expected to eventually reach ~50% (10x pre-IPO level); XAI-merger shares face a separate lockup until February 2nd under Rule 144's 12-month holding period.
5. Kevin's technical read: first Fibonacci retracement test at $133.34; he prefers an extension drawn from the bottom, and says if the stock falls to $80-86 that would be "a nicer place to buy," though he's not saying avoid buying in the low $100s.
6. Central risk thesis: Elon is projecting SpaceX compute rental rates of $30-40 billion/gigawatt (per Anthropic/Google deals) vs. a market rate Kevin puts at $10-15 million/megawatt — a 3-5x premium — while Neoclouds (Iron, Coreweave) rent comparable GB300 compute for $9-15 million/megawatt. Specific rate comparison: SpaceX GB300 rate ~$11/hour vs. ~$3/hour (IREN/Microsoft, Nvidia/Sharon AI deals) and ~$4/hour Iron/Nvidia rates in May; SpaceX's blended H100/H200 rate is ~$5/hour.
7. Kevin says the "availability premium" explanation is weak since Iron/Coreweave also have expanding capacity but don't command similar premiums; both Google and Anthropic have circular incentives to support SpaceX's valuation (Google's $94B SpaceX exposure, Google's Anthropic investment), and the contracts carry 90-day cancellation rights, adding renewal risk. Coreweave revenue is expected to grow from $13B to $33B; ARR from ~$551B(sic, likely $5-1B range misspoken) to $7-9B by end of 2026; IREN projects $4B ARR by end of 2026.
8. Bottom line risk: if SpaceX's premium rate is not sustainable or doesn't get renewed, its compute-revenue projections are "off by a multiple" and the stock's re-rating is premature; conversely, if the SpaceX rate becomes the real market benchmark, Neoclouds like Iron/Coreweave would have room to reprice upward, making them potentially the better investment. Kevin states he's not bearish on SpaceX (he holds exposure and benefits if it rises) but is flagging these as unresolved risk factors, not a call to sell.
