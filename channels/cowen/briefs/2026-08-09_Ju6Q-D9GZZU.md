## Key points (the distilled video)
1. Total non-farm payroll missed badly: expected ~80,000, actual print was 23,000; year-over-year change has not gone negative but came close in February and margin for error is now very thin (growth has fallen from ~5%/year in 2022 to roughly 2% now).
2. Despite the weak payroll print, the unemployment rate has fallen from 4.5% (last November) to 4.1%, explained mainly by a shrinking labor force participation rate (62.5% down to 61.4% since November) rather than job growth.
3. Layoffs remain low — back to pre-pandemic levels, not recessionary — and initial claims recently printed ~189,000, the lowest in roughly 50 years, indicating companies are not aggressively cutting staff.
4. Labor market weakness is uneven: job openings trending down but showing recent resilience, temporary help services weakening then ticking back up; some sectors (e.g., altcoins) already feel recessionary while AI/semis remain strong despite a recent correction.
5. Regional unemployment data shows only scattered/patchy state-level increases, unlike 2008 or 2001 when the entire country deteriorated simultaneously — the market keeps "climbing the wall of worry" because there's always a silver lining somewhere.
6. GDP-weighted global unemployment rate has stalled and is ticking back down; global inflation has cooled from 2022 peaks but is not yet back to 2% targets, complicating the rate-hike debate.
7. Market-implied odds of a September rate hike stand at only 43.9%, with the 2-year yield now sitting above the Fed funds rate — historically (e.g., the 1990s) the Fed eventually "chases" the 2-year yield if it doesn't fall back down.
8. The US-minus-Japan interest rate differential has historically preceded recessions when it drops sharply, and Cowen's macro recession risk dashboard shows only a 0.16 risk reading — recession risk has been low for 4-5 years but he expects the business cycle to end "within the next couple of years."
9. Bitcoin dominance excluding stablecoins is still rising (near late-last-year highs); the apparent drop in dominance is solely due to rising stablecoin dominance — a sign investors are favoring "blue chip" crypto/risk assets over riskier plays amid macro uncertainty.
10. Cowen expects S&P strength to continue near-term but forecasts a correction starting by late September at the latest, consistent with prior midterm years (2014, 2018, 2022), tied to the next Fed meeting on September 16th.

## Predictions made
- S&P 500: expects continued strength short-term but a correction to begin by "late September at the latest," in line with prior midterm years (2014, 2018, 2022); next Fed meeting September 16th cited as a catalyst window.
