## TL;DR
Kevin reads a mixed after-hours earnings batch (Seagate, Hood, Visa, Enphase all red). He likes Hood's business but would wait for a lower price (high-60s/70s) or a Bitcoin rally. He thinks Seagate's beat is strong and the stock looks cheap, but he wants the call first. He dismisses the BofA margin-debt warning as mid-cycle, not a market top.

## Main points
1. Seagate beat: revenue $4.2B vs $4.11B, adjusted EPS $7.68 vs $7.39, operating margin 50.7% vs 49.8%. Operating cash flow is about 3x last year with minimal capex. The stock still fell 6-8% after hours. By his math it trades at about 16x earnings on ~30% forecast growth, a roughly 0.5 PEG. He wants to hear the call before deciding.
2. Enphase had the worst print. Q4 revenue guide was $237M vs $298.8M expected (~20% miss), microinverters were 1.42M vs 1.46M, and battery shipments were 129.9 vs 140. He calls the guidance bad. He flags the 800V DC product commentary as the key item and calls the stock rate-sensitive.
3. Visa was a slight beat: payments revenue $12.1B vs $12.09B, EPS $3.48 vs $3.44. He called it "nothing crazy". Cheesecake beat: adjusted EPS $1.12 vs $0.95, revenue $1.03B vs $1.0B, operating income $75M vs $37M a year ago.
4. Hood missed on EPS ($0.51 vs $0.56) and revenue, and monthly active users were 13M vs 14.4M expected. He sees the MAU drop as the real red flag. Credit-loss provisions rose from $26M to $71M (+173%) and operating costs from $33M to $79M. He thinks the gold card (1.3M users, up from 800K in May) is a loss leader at 3% cash back.
5. Hood positives: transaction revenue up 19%, prediction markets up 61%, and the Rother prediction-market business at over $100M ARR. The balance sheet is strong. Crypto revenue was $120M vs $114M expected, though still down 38% year over year. Agentic trading (200K accounts) is only about $1.6M of revenue at 10 bps. His valuation math puts it at a PEG of about 2, only 5-10% undervalued. He is bullish on the business but would wait for a lower price, or for an Iran deal or Bitcoin rally. His view is that Hood tracks the Bitcoin cycle.
6. He calls the BofA/FINRA margin-debt warning a "nothing burger". Margin debt is still near record levels and the slowdown from May's pace is a hangover from the April leverage party. He thinks we are in a mid-cycle rally, not a permanent peak, and that credit spreads have peaked, which is bullish. He expects a post-midterms relief rally.
7. Market color: Bloom closed up over 7% and AMD up over 5%, both picks from his morning alpha report. NVDA was around $217 and TSLA near $414. Cerebras reportedly has a deal with Oracle. He sees Amazon's "anti-token-maxing" memo as expected cost discipline on AI spend. JetBlue surged on an upbeat forecast, which he reads as a short squeeze.
