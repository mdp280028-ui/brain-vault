## TL;DR
Kevin argues Elon Musk will "spend like a drunken sailor" on AI hardware regardless of ROI, and ranks nine hardware stocks by how much they benefit — with Nvidia, Marvell, and Lattice Semiconductor as his top three picks.

## Main points
1. Framing/disclosure: Kevin's fund has 7-figure SpaceX exposure bought at ~$330B valuation (now "up like 5x" on paper, shares not yet delivered from lockup); this video is not a SpaceX buy/sell call, just an exercise in identifying beneficiaries if Elon overspends on hardware.
2. Base case: current Foxconn order for Macro/Mini Hard is 13,000 racks (~$52.5B spend), already placed. Bull case from a SemiAnalysis piece pegs orders at 68,800 racks (5.3x), implying $275B–$320B of spend; Kevin criticizes the piece's inconsistency in assuming Elon earns $15–20/GPU hour while Anthropic pays only $3/GPU hour.
3. Kevin's core thesis: Elon doesn't care about ROI or earnings — he'll expand hardware supply because he's "drunk on the idea" of data centers, space, and Optimus robots, and will keep borrowing to fund it as long as the stock stabilizes.
4. Nvidia (top pick): direct deal with Elon confirmed; 75% gross margin, 71.4% net margin on $81.6B revenue (annualized ~$324B). Only up 20% YTD despite being the largest SpaceX beneficiary. If Nvidia captures the full $320B bull-case spend, that's an extra $100–150B revenue (~30% above 2028 estimates); if Reuben ramps and Elon reaches 10GW spend, total spend could hit $400–480B, potentially raising Nvidia's 2028 estimates by up to 50%. Fair value $795–850 (mid-2s to high-2s PEG); current PEG 0.76 on 34% forward growth, 25x forward PE — "dirt freaking cheap" in Kevin's words. Vera Rubin CPU line adds a new $20B/year TAM (Agentic AI orchestration) that isn't priced in yet.
5. Marvell (#2 pick): makes custom silicon (XPUs/ASICs) for AWS/Microsoft; NVLink Fusion lets Marvell's chips integrate with Nvidia's ecosystem, reducing exclusion risk. Potential (unconfirmed) beneficiary of Terafab chip needs. Fair value ~$370 (69% upside), 35% forward growth, PEG ~1.43. Optics/interconnect transition (if Terafab goes optics-based rather than Ethernet) is a further upside catalyst.
6. Lattice Semiconductor (LSCC, #3 pick): small-cap FPGA maker; each server rack has embedded FPGAs (1–2 currently, expected to rise to 3–4), worth $1,500–5,000 of Lattice content per rack. PEG 0.42 at current growth assumptions (doubling EPS); Kevin models fair value range of $225 (bear, 40–50% growth) to $763 (bull, ~100% growth) — high uncertainty due to lack of 4-year estimates.
7. Vertive (VRT): liquid cooling/data-center infrastructure play; each GB300 rack draws 125kW (2.5x average household hourly usage). Operating margins ~18.8% and rising, gross margins ~37%. Forward EPS $6.68, growth 23–28%/yr; fair value modeled at $390 (range $270–390) using 28.2% growth assumption; stock currently near $270. Order backlog extending to 12–18 month delivery windows; called an "Nvidia proxy."
8. Eaton (ETN): data center orders up 85% YoY, leading 800V DC transition, but balance sheet is weak — ~$9.3B current debt plus ~$12B long-term debt against only ~$700-800M cash, buying back stock at all-time highs while borrowing. PE ~33x, PEG ~2.1 on 15.7% growth and 17% margins — "not much room left."
9. Schneider Electric (OTC ADR): data centers only ~20% of revenue; EPS must be divided by 5 for ADR ($11.76 → $2.35), giving ~30x PE, PEG ~2 on 14.6% growth, 13-14% net margins. Fair value estimate ~$58 — downside from current price, lowest-conviction name.
10. Dell: risk flagged that SpaceX may skip Dell and order direct from Foxconn for GB300 servers per a Wells Fargo report; AI server margins are low (mid-single-digit%) though high volume. Stock at $453 near highs; PEG ~1.26 on 19.4% average growth (Kevin flags Dell's own platform underestimates growth at 10% vs. a more realistic ~19%), ~8% margins — valuation hard to justify much upside from here.
11. NextEra... correction, NPhase (Enphase, ENPH): ~7% free cash flow yield, highest among the group (vs. Nvidia ~2%, Marvell/Palantir ~1%, Eaton ~2%, Dell ~2%, Vertive ~2.5%). IQ solid-state transformers seen as a 2028 data-center opportunity, still early (RFP stage). At ~$42, PE ~20.5x on 18.6% growth = PEG ~1.1; 27% net margins; Kevin's fair value ~$70-77, calling it a value/cash-flow play tied to eventual rate cuts, not a near-term mover.
12. ARM: earns CPU-core licensing fees (not GPU-based), with related-party China revenue creating volatility; moving into custom silicon hardware (cash-flow risk) for OpenAI/Meta, ramping 2027, AGI CPU revenue not expected until fiscal 2031. At $282 vs. $2.23 EPS estimate (Mar 2027), PE ~126x, PEG ~2.67 on 47.4% growth — expensive, lowest upside of the group alongside Schneider.
