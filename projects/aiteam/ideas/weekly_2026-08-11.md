## Alert operator same-day when daily token cost exceeds 3x the trailing 7-day moving average

**Score:** 3.600000000000000088 · **Build:** 3.0h · **Impact:** `medium` · **Slug:** `token-cost-spike-anomaly-alert`

**Signal:** `diagnose.l_token_cost_trajectory_30d (2026-08-09: cost=$50.09, ma7=$11.93, a 4.2x spike against POLICY.md Q2's $25/day soft-cap)`

diagnose.l_token_cost_trajectory_30d shows 2026-08-09 at $50.09/day against a trailing 7-day moving average (ma7) of $11.93 — a 4.2x spike, the single largest daily cost in the entire 30-day window and more than 3x the $25/day soft-cap threshold locked in POLICY.md Q2. The prior day (2026-08-08) was $9.49 and the following day (2026-08-10, partial) dropped to $0.44, meaning this was a sharp one-day anomaly, not a sustained ramp the existing $25 soft-warning / $50 hard-stop caps were designed to catch gradually. The $50.09 figure sits almost exactly at the $50 hard-stop line, meaning it either triggered the hard-stop right at the boundary or narrowly missed it — either way, the operator has no anomaly-specific alert distinguishing 'one day mysteriously 4x normal' from 'gradual creep toward the cap,' which is a materially different failure mode requiring different diagnosis (a specific misbehaving agent that day vs. fleet-wide organic growth).

The build adds a same-day comparison step to whatever cron already computes the daily token_usage rollup: if today's cost exceeds 3x the trailing 7-day ma7, fire a Telegram alert naming the day's top-3 agents by cost (already computable from the same token_usage table diagnose.b already queries) so the operator can identify the specific runaway agent within minutes instead of discovering it days later during the next idea-agent synth run, which is how this spike was surfaced now, one day after the fact.

This prevents delayed discovery of single-day cost anomalies. Frequency: 1 occurrence in the 30-day window on record (2026-08-09), so frequency is roughly ~1/month at current observed rate — genuinely unknown whether this recurs, stated honestly rather than inflated. Impact if it recurs undetected: up to $50/day (the hard-stop ceiling) burned before the existing cap intervenes, versus same-day detection which could catch it within hours if the alert runs on a tighter cron than the daily rollup.

Downside: if the 3x-ma7 threshold is too sensitive relative to the fleet's natural day-to-day variance (the 30-day data shows swings from $0.09 to $17.14 even without anomalies, e.g., 2026-07-24 to 2026-07-25), this could produce false-positive alerts on ordinary high-activity days, training the operator to ignore the alert — the threshold would need one or two weeks of tuning against real variance before being trusted.
