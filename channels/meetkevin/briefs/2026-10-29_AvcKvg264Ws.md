## TL;DR
Kevin reads the Google and ServiceNow selloffs as company-specific problems, not the end of the AI buildout. He stays bullish on hardware and buys the dip. He expects all-time highs by Black Friday, helped by a dovish Fed and falling credit default swaps. He also turns bullish on a Tesla–SpaceX merger. The second half of the stream is a Duo (iPhone/iPad) device review.

## Main points
1. Iran news: Vance sent a letter to Iranian hardliners to restart peace talks, and Trump approved it. Russia offered to mediate, and Trump has threatened strikes after the midterms. Kevin reads this as good cop/bad cop and a deadline-setting move. Oil is at $113, off the 117 peak, and the 10-year is down ~6bp, still around 5%.
2. Data: Q3 GDP came in at 3.4% vs 3.2% expected, and the GDP price index at 2.3 vs 3.2. Personal spending was 0.5% vs 0.6%, and personal income 0.2% vs 0.3%. He sees weakening worker pricing power, with 60,000 Microsoft buyouts as a risk. The labor market is still absorbing layoffs, so he is "worried about the labor market" but sees no recession yet.
3. Rate hikes: after the dovish Fed (Warsh) meeting, expected hikes through Sept 2027 fell from 3.1 to 2.5. He thinks that means only one hike in the cycle. The Kalshi "one hike" bet is only at 21¢, and he thinks it jumps to 50-80% with an Iran deal. The bear counter is that 5-year breakeven inflation is rising.
4. Credit default swaps (CDS) peaked around Oct 13 and are falling. Oracle's CDS is below its Oct 13 level and its free cash flow is expected to turn positive sooner. CoreWeave's CDS fell from ~1150 to 989, and Meta's has stabilized. Microsoft's CDS is stable, at about a third of Nvidia's pricing, despite a $25B bond raise. He says the market has missed that the fear already peaked.
5. Rotation: the Nasdaq is weak (QQQ lost 715, rejected at 715 after 20 minutes, then recovered), while the equal-weight S&P is green. He called Eli Lilly (up ~8% on a 6.4% guidance raise and an EPS beat of ~7.50 vs ~5.50-5.90), Apple and Moderna in his premarket Alpha report, all three rose. His pattern: healthcare and Apple do well on "suckening" days (large AI capital raises: Meta $96B, Broadcom $63B, SpaceX $40B plus $40B, Microsoft $25B).
6. ServiceNow fell ~24% (a record drop) on subscription costs up 58% against revenue up ~20.5%. He had flagged pricing power in July and avoided it. He says the problem is ServiceNow-specific and Google's is search disruption, so neither is market-wide. A CNBC analyst cited a 40%+ rally off the lows.
7. Bottom line: AI is "absolutely not" over, and the euphoric rally is ahead. He is very bullish on hardware (Nvidia, SK Hynix, Seagate, Western Digital) and on buying the QQQ or its components through year-end. He calls the QQQ 700-707 the buy-the-dip zone and 715 the current line. He expects all-time highs by Black Friday and higher by year-end, while admitting he could be wrong.
8. Elon/SpaceX: Musk says 20 gigawatts next year is "not 0%". Kevin estimates $420B more is needed for 10 GW and about $600B for the next 10, roughly $1T, with ~60% going to Nvidia. SpaceX has only ~$30B of free cash after debt, so he thinks a Tesla–SpaceX merger is the only way to raise it. Kalshi odds: 64% before 2027, 74% before 2028.
9. Valuation: SpaceX trades at ~144-169 on a 0.38 PEG, with a $1.9T cap. Tesla is at ~405 on a 4.9 PEG, with a $1.61T cap. The market-cap-weighted combined PEG is 2.6, and he thinks it is closer to 2. He bought Tesla around 360 and is buying volatility on the combination. He sees 132 as a floor for SpaceX.
10. Private credit and Alphabet: an Ares executive says non-accruals are ~1.5-2% and the economy is strong, with $5T+ of AI capex demand. Alphabet (-12% on CNBC) had free cash flow of -$9.1B, and Amazon reports tonight with AWS growth expected around 38% and capex of $220B. A $11B SpaceX stock loss hit Alphabet's net income.
11. Apple/Duo review (non-signal tech segment): after 6 days he loves the matte-screen Duo, which he says "will be the upgrade cycle for Apple". He also says "very bullish for Apple stock" and that he owns it. He also covers 64 GB laptops for agentic AI work and hardware security keys.
