## TL;DR
Kevin says the new BLS jobs report (-23,000 vs. an 80,000 survey, plus a 103,000 two-month downward revision) confirms a weakening labor trend he'd flagged in ADP data weeks ago, and argues AI is now "killing more jobs than it's creating" — a late-cycle warning sign that raises longer-term downside risk even though it lowers the odds of a Fed rate hike.

## Main points
1. Kevin references a chart he posted ~2.5 weeks ago showing ADP weekly employment data trending down; weak/crap ADP prints in late last year (partly Trump-related uncertainty, partly "Liberation Day") gave way to a hiring boom after IEEPA tariffs died and a "pre-Hormuz" ceasefire took hold, pushing ADP hiring up to ~40,000, before plummeting on a trend line to ~15,000 and now, per the latest ADP report, to ~10,000.
2. Today's BLS jobs report: survey expected 80,000, actual came in at -23,000 — Kevin says this "basically kills the idea for a rate hike for now," though he cautions there's still more data (two more CPI reports, another jobs report) before drawing firm conclusions.
3. The prior month's report was revised down 57,000 (from a reported 77,000 to 20,000), making the two-month revision a combined -103,000.
4. Private payrolls also missed: 30,000 actual vs. 82,000 survey; the prior private payrolls print was revised down from 49,000 to 30,000.
5. Average hourly earnings rose just 0.1% month-over-month and 3.2% year-over-year (vs. 3.5% expected) — Kevin calls this evidence workers "have no pricing power anymore," partly attributing it to competition from AI/robots.
6. Unemployment rate fell, but only because labor force participation fell — household survey shows 87,000 fewer people in the workforce and 178,000 fewer job seekers; participation rate is down 1.1% since July 2025, which Kevin notes equals roughly 1.5 million people given a ~150 million-worker base. People not in the labor force but wanting a job: 5.9 million; part-time for economic reasons: 4.8 million.
7. Kevin says the participation-rate decline is "starting to tank vertically," a pattern he says is rare outside of COVID, and states he personally believes there's "glaring evidence that artificial intelligence at the moment is killing more jobs than it's creating."
8. He cites a CNBC segment (aired on TV but reportedly cut from CNBC's YouTube upload) in which a Wells Fargo executive said the bank is "focused on growing revenues and returns" by becoming more efficient via AI, displacing workers, and acknowledged there may be a persistent gap where reskilled workers still can't find enough jobs even if they catch up on skills.
9. Sector detail from the report: government is firing; healthcare hiring is well below trend; retail trade was -19 (per government data); ADP data this week was also weak, with a weakening trend rather than an outright warning to "run for cover." Leisure and hospitality job losses are seen as a likely temporary/limited effect of post-FIFA event layoffs.
10. Kevin's bottom line: this is a warning sign that the AI cycle (not Ray Dalio's multi-decade government debt cycle) is in its "late innings" over roughly the next 3-5 years, which "increases our downside risk and should make us more conservative" on debt exposure — not a signal to stop investing.
11. He notes the weak jobs data lowers the risk of a Fed rate hike, but flags that the 10-year Treasury, despite falling 5 basis points, remains above 4.6%, and Brent crude remains above $81 — both of which he says "still sucks" / "isn't great."
12. Closing view: the ceasefire-driven hiring boom may itself have been the real "blip," not the current weakening trend; when unemployment eventually rises, Kevin expects "the market's going to start panicking about a recession."
