## TL;DR
Kevin stays bullish on Nvidia (worth well over $300 eventually) and keeps his QQQ call of 800 by March 31 and all-time highs by January 1. He says the AI boom is early and the bubble pop comes later, as an unemployment recession.

## Main points
1. The market is "kooky dookie" with too much leverage, and leveraged ETFs "will go to zero". He cites FINRA margin debt at its highest in September and second highest in October. He prefers cheap index funds like MGK (5 bps), though he finds Apple and Nvidia at 13% each too top-heavy.
2. He explains thin order books. Only about 1–3% of a stock trades daily (Tesla 1%, Nvidia 3%), so one panic market order can reprice the whole company. His extreme example is a $200 stock with no bids until $118, a 41% drop.
3. On a new IPO he calls "Ant", he expects a floor of about $77. His lines are 10–20% below the $97.17 close, at $87.45 and $77.74. If it breaks lower, he says it could drag down the Nasdaq.
4. Individual names: CrowdStrike is down 10% on an all-stock Zscaler deal, which he calls overpaying and dilutive. AMD sits at his $452 line. SanDisk is oversold. He has been a "dirty bear" on memory as cyclical, but a 2025 retracement near $26.80 is "not bad". Reddit faces risk from AI search and its log-in-only move.
5. Nvidia: he does not think the bounce is a bull trap and says it is worth "well over $300" eventually, with no date. He says the AI bubble is very early. A later pop would become an unemployment recession, which he says the labor data doesn't show now. He plans to watch ADP weekly (last week 23.9K) as the first November read.
6. QQQ: he keeps the 800 target by March 31 and all-time highs by January 1. He won't accelerate it, because testimony and Fed worries will keep spooking people.
7. Oil: Brent is back under $90 and WTI under $80, on talk of a 2027 supply glut. Hormuz disruption has pushed producers to add supply. Neoclouds face harder financing, though CoreWeave credit default swaps are falling and the stock is back near its IPO price.
8. Bargains: he likes Corning and Lumentum (owns both), calls Bloom Energy a dip-buy, and sees Dell's enterprise buildout as a theme. Enphase is his "year of" 2027 yolo at about $2.7B market cap. He estimates about $200M annual cash flow, a 7.2% yield, and the company is cutting 12% of staff. He suggests a 1–2% position.
9. Real estate: he calls it a fantastic time to buy. He says 10-year yield peaked at 5.27%, and single-family building permits are collapsing, which points to a sturdier bottom in Texas and Florida within about two years. He favors coastal or landlocked, Democrat-run, and luxury areas. Buying is hard now because more money down is needed. For net worth under $500K he still says real estate.
10. Bitcoin: BTC is under $60K after about $1B of liquidations in 24 hours. It lost $82K, and he expects recovery only in the next real stock boom once the "AI is a bubble" fear fades. He also puts a global conflict before 2028 at about 10%.
11. Key-man risk: he says Elon and Jensen carry it. He puts Jensen's chance of dying within a year at roughly 1.4%. He defends Cathie Wood's Nvidia sale as a founder-loyalty move. He notes Michael Burry sold most of his Nvidia puts after the 31% jump. He calls Google's cancelled chip orders a shift from inference to training chips, not a problem.
12. Planes and recession: he sold his jet for more than he paid and says the next buy is the next recession. His example is a $13M plane at about $5M in 2032 with zero rates. He plans to borrow again only then. He also calls AI-driven job loss ahead, which is why he is anti-debt. Promotion segments are skipped.
