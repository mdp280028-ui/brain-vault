## TL;DR
Despite the "depression" title, Kevin says the AI buildout is only beginning. Big Tech off-balance-sheet AI commitments hit $4.11T, rising GPU rental prices and hardware margins support it, and he says to buy the dip in hardware (AMD below 520). Token sellers are the weak business model, and the labor market is holding up (JOLTS beat).

## Main points
1. Labor market pillar: September JOLTS showed 7.523M vacancies vs 7.1M expected, and the prior month was revised up 1.5%. The quits rate rose from 1.9% to 2.1%. He says less than half of quitters now get a raise, so it is not a post-COVID hot market, but he calls the data good. Kalshi odds of only one rate hike fell from 27-28% to 24%, which means the market is pricing a second hike. He also notes that Warsh is shrinking the balance sheet, so he doesn't expect money printing.
2. AI demand is accelerating: Big Tech AI commitments are $4.11T, vs 3.1T in August and 1.8T in June, more than doubled in 5 months. The 5 hyperscalers burned $31.4B of free cash flow in Q3 on $230B of capex (+118%). Morgan Stanley forecasts $195B of lease capex in 2027 and $246B in 2028. He calls AMD's new $4.7B debt a sign that spending is still ramping. He says this means "more at the beginning of the journey."
3. Compute pricing: token prices are down ~50% y/y (index from ~$2 to ~$0.91), but H100 rental prices are up 6.2% since May 31 to ~$1.30/hr. B200 and B300 prices are rising faster, and even 4.5-year-old chips are still gaining. He reads this as AI getting more useful. It also supports CoreWeave and Nebius debt.
4. Winners and losers: AMD gross margin was 54% vs 51.7% a year ago, with data center revenue almost doubling, sequential growth of 15.9% guided to 27%, and free cash flow of only $554M. Apple's product gross margin was 39.3% vs 36.8% despite higher memory prices. Intel missed because PC demand fell (AMD client segment -8%), which he says is not an AI problem. He argues that selling tokens is a losing business, and that winners sell hardware or become "SaaS 2.0" platforms (Palantir, Salesforce). Anthropic designing its own chips is evidence of this.
5. Valuation (PEG): Nvidia 0.67, Broadcom 0.63, Micron 0.33, AMD ~1.2 (twice Nvidia's), Apple ~2 to almost 4, Palantir ~2.1. He thinks Apple will grow 16-20% vs the Street's 9%. He expects NAND, Seagate and SK Hynix to be the next leg, after the leveraged memory trade was flushed out. His basket: Marvell, Broadcom, AMD, Apple, Nvidia, a little Microsoft, Amazon, SpaceX and Tesla, with no Google. He owns Palantir but calls the earnings-call analyst questions weak and sees competition coming.
6. Risks: OpenAI's raise was cut from $30B at $1.4T to $20B at $1.25T. Hartnett projects AI capex at $1.2T/yr in 2027, with free cash flow going from +$240B to -$180B. Oracle's 40-year bond yields 8.43% vs Microsoft's 6.08%. CoreWeave's CDS bounced but made no new highs. Four of the Mag 7 are in a bear market, and 60%+ of S&P stocks are more than 20% below their highs. Deutsche Bank sees up to ~5% more downside until Iran clears. He sees Democrats winning the midterms (Senate 74%, House 93%) and prediction markets show voters don't want data centers.
7. Conclusion: he says "the boom is just beginning" and the market is too pessimistic, calling hardware "the same category" as real estate, where pessimism creates opportunity. He is buying the dip, and his own split is ~90% real estate and 5-10% stocks.
