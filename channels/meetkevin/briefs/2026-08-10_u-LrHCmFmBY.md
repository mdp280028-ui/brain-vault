## TL;DR
Kevin argues the Iran war is escalating, not winding down, which will keep defense contract flow strong — but he still calls RTX and LMT overpriced, debt-laden, and due for a pullback (54% and 33% downside to his fair value estimates, respectively).

## Main points
1. Escalation, not resolution: Pentagon's leaked "Fineberg directive" (Aug 5, deputy defense secretary Steve Feinberg) orders defense firms to propose accelerated production plans within 21 days after the US burned through 850 Tomahawks, 1,000 Patriot/THAAD interceptors, and ~1,300 other ballistic missiles in the war's first 30 days — those 850 Tomahawks took 14 years (since 2012) to build. Each THAAD interceptor costs $10-12M.
2. Trump is publicly "low-keying" the war (per Axios interview) while privately floating walking away from a nuclear deal to declare victory (per WSJ) ahead of midterms in 85 days; Kevin thinks this signals the war drags on much longer, not toward resolution.
3. RTX financials: net sales +14.45% YoY, costs +12.75%; only $8.3B cash + $14B receivables vs $63B of bills due — "basically broke" despite being a $300B company; long-term debt adds another $32B+$7.3B. Bomb margins 12.6% vs commercial engine (Pratt & Whitney) margins 8.3%; Collins Aerospace aftermarket margins 15.9%. Net income +33%/31.5% (6mo basis), FCF yield ~2.8%. Paying ~$1B debt paydown + ~$2B dividends in 6 months despite the balance sheet strain.
4. LMT financials: sales +10.5% with flat costs (margin expansion), driven by shift to higher-margin F-35 deliveries; $59B deal to triple Patriot production over 7 years. $7B cash+receivables vs $11.6B bills plus $29B other long-term debt; pension liabilities roughly equal to cash. Net earnings +61% YoY (6mo), FCF yield ~3.9%, $2.6B FCF in 6 months (~$5.2B annualized) not being reinvested.
5. Valuation call: both overpriced. LMT trades at 19x forward earnings / 7% growth = 2.75 PEG, "high for a company with 9% margins" — 33% downside to fair value. RTX has ~3.0 PEG on 8% margins/9% growth — 54% downside to fair value; balance sheet flagged as a yellow-flag risk on Kevin's stock AI tool. RSI: RTX at 71 (overbought, weekly), LMT at 58 (less extended).
6. Trump signed executive order 14372, effectively banning dividends/buybacks for "underperforming" defense contractors (defined broadly — includes failing to reinvest cash flow or missing deadlines) with immediate enforcement via contract clauses, no Congress needed — direct response to RTX paying dividends despite its debt load.
7. Broader Middle East risk stack: Iran now escalating demands beyond April's nuclear-deal terms (wants Gaza/Yemen/Iraq/Lebanon conflicts ended, blockade and sanctions lifted, full reparations) just to reopen the Strait of Hormuz; ships still being attacked nightly near Hormuz despite US attempts to route tankers via Oman; Houthis struck Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery and killed 58 Yemeni government troops; Israel (BB, election Oct 27) publicly rejected Trump's Gaza deal, raising odds Israel strikes Iran independently over the "Pickaxe Mountain" site.
8. Oil and macro: Brent trading $80-85 (vs ~$70 high/$59 low late last year); Kevin expects prolonged inflationary pressure over the next 6+ months from the conflict, and flags increased (though still "coin toss") odds of a Fed rate hike in September as a result. Bank of America bull/bear indicator at 9.7, a "screaming sell" level last seen Dec 2020/Oct 2025/Feb 2026 — Kevin reads this as broad market froth, not specific to defense names.
