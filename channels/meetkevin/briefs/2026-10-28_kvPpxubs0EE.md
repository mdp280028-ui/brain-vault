## TL;DR
Google's earnings were a real miss and it fell ~7-9% after hours, because its biggest business, search ads, is slowing. Microsoft beat and rose ~3-5%. Kevin holds Microsoft and avoids Google. He reads this as a Google-specific problem, not the end of the AI boom. He is bullish on the rumored Marvell–Lumentum deal and would buy the Marvell dip.

## Main points
1. Microsoft Q1 beat: revenue $92.2B vs $90.6B, EPS $4.89 vs $4.73, Azure +48% vs 44% expected, cloud revenue $63.9B vs $61.62B. Stock was up ~3-5% after hours. Commercial RPO rose 73% and the backlog is $677B.
2. Capex looked "lower" at $39.37B vs $45.93B expected. He says Microsoft didn't really slow capex. It reclassified it through leases, upfront payments to neoclouds and longer depreciation lives. Free cash flow of $19B falls to roughly zero once the other investments (~$13B and $5.3B) are included. He holds Microsoft anyway but flags this.
3. Microsoft's operating income grew 20.9% vs revenue 18.8%, which looks like pricing power. He distrusts that, because leases were stretched from 15 to 25 years. He says this likely removed $2-3B of depreciation, so pricing power is "stable at best".
4. Google's headline numbers missed. Revenue was $127.5B vs $127.93B, search $63.35B vs $64.85B (-2.3%), ad revenue -1.9%, YouTube -1.9% (+9.2% growth in an election year), services -2.1%, operating income -6%, and EPS missed (~29% on his notes). Cloud beat at $30.7B vs $29.1B (+103% growth). The stock fell ~7-9% to about $291-293.
5. Why it matters: ~75% of Google revenue is ads, and ~73% of that is search and websites. He suspects the "query growth at a 5-year high" claim counts AI Overview and AI Mode chats, which carry no sponsored results. That makes it self-cannibalizing. A guest analyst adds that search growth went 19% in March, 17% in June, 12% now, and guidance points lower.
6. Google's balance sheet is not a bankruptcy risk: cash is about $256B, with $125B of bills and $191B of long-term debt. It issued $29B of debt and $12.3B of stock this quarter, and free cash flow was -$9B vs +$25B a year ago. Capex guide went up 4% to $195-205B, a ~1% miss against hopes for more. It disclosed $1T+ in lease and AI purchase commitments, RPO is $561B, and there is a $7.6B alphabet-level AI R&D loss.
7. His conclusion is that Google is being disrupted by AI, while the AI buildout itself is "full speed ahead" and Google isn't a canary. He cites SK Hynix's comment that one customer faltering won't slow industry spend. He is diversifying across AI hardware and enterprise names to avoid single-name risk.
8. Bessent interview (aired in the stream): he calls the energy price shock transitory and says the Fed should keep an open mind on hikes. He expects a strong Q3 GDP (Atlanta Fed well over 3%). He says the Iran blockade has cut Iran's oil exports to zero and the economy may contract 25%. He sees neoclouds' junk debt as not problematic and the trade deals as largely locked in.
9. Rumor: Marvell is in advance talks to buy Lumentum (~$83B) as soon as next week. Marvell is ~$221B, fell to about $226 after the report, and Lumentum rose ~10%. Marvell lacks the cash (about $6B free cash), so the deal means debt and dilution. Kevin sees it as vertical integration: lasers, silicon photonics, DSPs, ASICs and NVLink Fusion access, with Nvidia having put $2B into each company.
10. Marvell vs Broadcom: Marvell bets on fiber and co-packaged optics because of a "copper wall" (copper can't scale beyond ~3m). Broadcom (~60% of the ASIC market vs Marvell's 15-25%) and Jensen prefer copper while it works. Kevin owns both Marvell and Broadcom. He is annoyed he didn't buy Lumentum earlier, when it was at a 1.2 PEG with revenue up 1.8x and gross profit up 2.7x.
11. He would buy the Marvell dip because he wants the combined company, though he flags execution risk. He is positive on Corning (~$113B, 1.39 PEG, only half the business is booming) but doesn't own it. He leans into AI hardware broadly but wants diversification. He expects a "hardware 2.0" rally after an Iran deal. ServiceNow is falling hard (a guest says it went from $240 to $100).
