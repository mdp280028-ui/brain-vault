# Evidence log: Labor Market & Unemployment

Append-only. Newest entries at bottom.

- 2026-05-08 (APCjkL5gfFI): The May 2026 jobs report shows no recession signal — unemployment steady at 4.3%, initial claims well below the analyst's explicit 300K threshold, and layoffs near pre-pandemic levels. Non-farm payrolls are still growing YoY (~250K) but the YoY rate is approaching zero, and JOLTS data shows openings below available workers with low quits. Cowen ties the labor market's continued resilience directly to elevated asset prices: "one of the things you have to have in order to have a recession and for the business cycle to end is you have to have lower asset prices" [02:42]. A seasonal uptick in claims — and a likely modest rise in the unemployment rate — is expected by late summer 2026 based on confirmed patterns from 2023–2025.

- 2026-05-27 (Ibh7wDgNGCE): Cowen uses the labor market explicitly to explain why SPX climbed back to all-time highs after the early-year drop — with layoffs and initial claims still low, there is no self-reinforcing feedback loop, and "the market loves to climb the wall of worry until there is a sufficient reason not to do so, and we haven't had that sufficient reason just yet" [01:23]. The unemployment map shows rising unemployment across many states over the past 6 months, but Cowen notes that prior recessions required the entire country to be affected — that nationwide threshold has not been crossed. The labor market's status as the gating condition for further equity downside is reaffirmed: a sustained SPX decline would be required to trigger the feedback loop.

- 2026-08-09 (Ju6Q-D9GZZU): Non-farm payroll missed badly (23,000 actual vs. ~80,000 expected), yet the unemployment rate fell from 4.5% to 4.1% because labor-force participation dropped from 62.5% to 61.4%, not because of job growth. Layoffs remain at pre-pandemic levels and initial claims printed ~189,000, the lowest in roughly 50 years, so Cowen maintains his recession threshold: "as long as initial claims are below 300K, we're just not in a recession" [23:48]. State-level unemployment increases remain scattered rather than nationwide, and his macro recession risk dashboard reads a low 0.16.
