## TL;DR
Kevin sold his entire Circle (CRCL) position — not over earnings or OUSD, but because the Fed's FedNow instant-payment system is killing the core bull case (instantaneous 24/7 domestic transactions), and he thinks Circle risks becoming "a Sears."

## Main points
1. Kevin sold all Circle stock at ~$61 vs. a ~$70 cost basis (a small loss), after the stock had run up as high as ~$120 at one point; the sale was flagged live to his Alpha membership before this video.
2. The threat isn't earnings (revenue missed by 1.6%, USDC circulation up 25% YoY, EPS in line) or OUSD (a 140-partner consortium that would pass reserve income to partners like Visa instead of Circle shareholders) — Kevin says both are survivable and USDC remains the #2 stablecoin with no real competitor gaining traction.
3. The real threat is the Federal Reserve's FedNow real-time payments system, which he warned about in a December 2022 video ("I wrote crypto to zero, the Fed's new crypto replacement") — he thought it would take a decade to matter but says it's now arriving fast: 1,800+ banks/credit unions on it, 7 of top 10 banks live, half of US checking/savings accounts have access, transactions up 85% YoY (though off a small base, ~30K/day, ~11M/year), average transaction size $101,000.
4. FedNow offers free, instant, 24/7/365 domestic transfers (his bank gave him early access; he tested sending $2,500 instantly) with limits of $100K–$10M, directly undercutting stablecoins' core "instantaneous 24/7" value proposition domestically; he thinks it will also eventually absorb Zelle (2,600 banks) since FedNow adoption is growing faster and Zelle's transaction limits are clunky.
5. Valuation concern: Circle is a ~$14B company with ~$1.5B free cash and ~$3.5B shareholder equity, trading at roughly 4-5x book value and ~62x forward earnings (~$1 EPS expected by year-end) — a multiple he says needs a strong growth narrative to justify, and he believes that narrative (instantaneous payments) just died. Revenue grew only 6.5% while USDC circulation grew 25%, which he calls a sign of weak pricing power (partly due to Fed rate cuts).
6. He dismisses two bull-case rebuttals: (a) Circle's new banking charter is a limited trust bank for holding assets, not a commercial lending license, so it doesn't open new revenue; (b) the CEO's smart-contract/agentic-AI payments pitch doesn't hold up because humans will always want a human in the loop, and rules-based automation can just run inside existing banking apps/portals — the rail (stablecoin vs. FedNow) won't matter.
7. Stablecoins still retain an edge in cross-border/international payments for now, but Kevin expects FedNow-style rails (which already use SWIFT-compatible ISO 20022 language) to eventually extend there too; stock settlement is also moving to T+0 via bank-side real-time gross settlement (RTGS), bypassing stablecoins entirely.
8. Spillover risk flagged for PayPal and Block/Venmo, since FedNow could replace peer-to-peer app transfers by letting users send instantly from their bank account with no extra app; he notes only ~5% of PayPal's revenue comes from Venmo (a bit more including PayPal P2P), tempering the read-through.
