## TL;DR
Kevin argues AI is "bifurcating" into a high-margin frontier-compute layer (Nvidia, AMD data-center chips) that's approaching an S-curve limit, versus a low-margin, commoditizing enterprise/inference layer — and says the real money going forward will be made in software companies that exploit cheap open-weight models, not in hardware.

## Main points
1. Meta is releasing an open-weight model ("Muse Glimmer") more powerful than its Meta Spark model, timed with Alibaba's Qwen 3.8 27B release this week; Kevin reads this as Zuckerberg repositioning Meta as pro-"AI for the masses" after falling behind on frontier AI, echoing Elon Musk's original open-source mission for OpenAI.
2. Core framework: AI spending splits into (a) frontier compute (Vera Rubin CPUs + Reuben GPUs / GB300 Blackwell clusters, ~$4M per rack) which gives Nvidia its highest margins, funded by trillionaire-scale buyers like Elon Musk and capital-constrained frontier labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft); and (b) inference/fine-tuning and enterprise AI, which is commoditized and increasingly served by cheaper open-weight models on consumer/business-grade chips.
3. Evidence of capex-hiding: Microsoft changed its depreciation schedule for leases from 15 to 25 years, reclassifying data-center/office leases as opex/COGS instead of capex, because heavy disclosed capex has been punishing stocks; Google has been off all-time highs while raising ~$40B in stock (not yet sold as of June 30) plus ~$25B from Berkshire Hathaway-linked financing to fund AI spend.
4. Enterprise AI (including Kevin's own company) runs mostly on open-weight models on Blackwell RTX 6000 (~$18,000) or 5090 (~$4,900) chips instead of $4M frontier racks; he estimates enterprise AI is only "about 3 months" behind frontier quality, and since the AI boom is ~36-40 months old, that makes enterprise ~90% caught up ("10% behind" time-wise).
5. Corsair case study (not a strong buy): sales down 2% but gross profit up 21%; 8% net margin, ~2 PEG, ~15.2 PE, EPS estimate 90 cents, growth ~7-10%; ~$98M free cash flow, ~$1.5B market cap; AI workstation revenue not expected until "late 2027." Kevin concludes hardware-assembly/consumer layers are too commoditized to be compelling investments.
6. Frontier-compute names (Nvidia, AMD, memory like Micron) remain reliant on continued frontier-lab spending, which is itself capped by valuation ceilings; Kevin cites a stockanalysis.com chart showing Anthropic's implied valuation falling from a peak of ~$1.4T to $826B, and says this creates urgency for Anthropic/OpenAI to IPO soon to keep raising money and sustain the hardware cycle.
7. Kevin states his opinion that AGI is "fugazi" (a lie) — AI today is asymptotically approaching a technological limit (S-curve), comparing it to Bitcoin issuance approaching zero; AI is good at pattern matching but bad at reasoning in novel scenarios, cross-domain knowledge transfer, self-recognizing hallucination, and goal management (when to stop/ask for help/restrategize).
8. Nvidia's cash flow shows $26.5B in one quarter spent on purchases of non-marketable/marketable securities (investments in Lancium, NBIS, Marvell, CoreWeave, Intel, Lumentum, Coherent, Synopsys, Nokia) — more than all of 2025 combined; Kevin calls this "circular financing" where Nvidia funds buyers who then buy Nvidia chips.
9. Kevin still believes a "second wave" of hardware spending is coming (from SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI money) and calls it a medium-term (~6-month) trade where Nvidia, Marvell, and LSCC "can still do quite well," but says the market is approaching the end of the high-margin hardware cycle, after which the shift to low-margin/commoditized enterprise hardware is not bullish for hardware — it benefits software instead.
10. He references timing a prior CPU-stock shift into AMD around $196 as an "early trade" made in his Alpha membership, drawing an analogy to now positioning early for a shift from hardware into software beneficiaries.
11. Named software beneficiaries: Palantir (calls current ~130 range "a good valuation," said he'd "back up the truck" at 89, notes technical convergence toward a 106 level where they added exposure before it broke through) and Axon (volatile, just broke and rejected the top of a "down channel"). He flags Palantir as short-term overbought (daily and weekly) despite the fundamental bull case, calling the recent surge "early" and "some trade momentum."
12. Kevin expects the hardware-to-software rotation to become visible in Q3/Q4 (through year-end) and says this dynamic is already showing up in his own real-estate/AI startup, which is prop-tech (not frontier AI) and benefits from cheap, commoditized AI hardware/models — the company holds ~$100M of no-bank-debt real estate and is building an AI "terminal" product for housing and stock analysis.
