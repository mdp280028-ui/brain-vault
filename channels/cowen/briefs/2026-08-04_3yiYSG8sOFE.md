## TL;DR
Cowen argues the S&P's new all-time highs fit the pattern of prior midterm years, where corrections don't start until mid-August to late-September (often around the September Fed meeting), and that a deeper second-half correction in stocks is what typically ushers in Bitcoin's market cycle bottom.

## Key points (the distilled video)
1. S&P is at new all-time highs (7750) on August 4th, consistent with Cowen's prior thesis that midterm years see new highs extending into the August/September window before any correction.
2. In the three most recent midterm years, corrections started September 19th (2014, ~10% drop), September 21st (2018, ~20% drop), and August 16th (2022, ~20% drop, but that was already a bear market) — all mid-to-late in the year, not earlier.
3. The next Fed meeting is September 16th, with roughly a coin-flip probability (57% hike / 43% no change); Cowen thinks a September hike is plausible since 2024 and 2025 rate cuts both first occurred in September, and argues the Fed may prefer to act in September rather than right before midterms or waiting until December.
4. Whether the Fed hikes or holds, Cowen sees a path to a correction: a hike could spook markets on fear the hiking cycle is restarting, while a hold could still see the 10-year and 30-year yields grind higher (30-year already back to October 2023 levels; 10-year at 4.6% heading that way), pressuring risk assets.
5. Cowen stresses the correction "starting" in August/September historically did not mean it "finished" then — 2018's correction didn't bottom until December, 2014's until mid-October, and 2022's until mid-October.
6. He frames the S&P analysis as a tool to estimate Bitcoin's market cycle bottom: in 2014, 2018, and 2022, Bitcoin didn't bottom until the stock market's second, later-year correction, not the earlier one — and 2026 has already had its "first correction" early in the year, mirroring that pattern.
7. Draws a fractal-style comparison between the current S&P pattern (low, rally, sideways near the bull market support band, push up) and Bitcoin's own chart last cycle, suggesting a deeper correction later this year that retests a longer-term trendline — possibly (not certainly) back toward 6,000 on the S&P.
8. If the S&P did fall that far, Cowen believes it would likely cost Bitcoin its ~60K support level; he hedges this is uncertain, citing 2018 where Bitcoin held $6,000 support most of the year despite the S&P's first correction only being 7-8%, until the second correction dragged Bitcoin down ~48%.
9. Notes the dollar (DXY) is tracking a similar path under the current Trump term as it did in 2017-2018, and if that continues, a later dollar rally could be a headwind causing renewed weakness in risk assets later in the midterm year.
10. Reiterates his standing personal approach: dollar-cost-averaging into low-expense-ratio index funds monthly, not trying to time market tops or bottoms, and viewing corrections as buying opportunities rather than sell signals.

## Predictions made
- S&P correction unlikely to begin before mid-August at the earliest, with Cowen leaning toward a September start (potentially tied to the September 16th Fed meeting), possibly extending into October.
- If the pattern matches 2018, the S&P correction could continue bottoming as late as December 2026.
- Bitcoin's market cycle bottom is expected to follow the stock market's second/later-year correction (i.e., after the August/September S&P correction plays out), not before.
- Possible (not confident) scenario: S&P retests a longer-term trendline back toward 6,000, which would likely break Bitcoin's ~60K support.
